# SheWellRX voice and terminology

How every sentence on shewellrx.com, in OptiMantra templates, and in patient email is written. Compiled 2026-09-02 from the language of Midi Health, Evernow, Alloy, Maven Clinic, Allara, and Tia, then set against New Jersey telehealth rules and the FTC's health-claim standards. The same guide is installed as the project skill `.claude/skills/shewellrx-voice/SKILL.md`.

## The one rule

**The patient is the subject of every sentence.** Her symptoms, her options, her plan, her privacy. The clinician appears as "your clinician" or "a board certified ObGyn", and as "Dr. Copur" only where a credential, a promise, or a legal statement needs a name. The practice is never the doctor's story.

- Never: "the women he treats", "he has treated menopause for 35 years", "Dr. Copur reads it before the visit".
- Instead: "the women in our care", "more than thirty five years of experience in menopause care", "your history is reviewed before the visit begins".

## Voice

Premium telehealth reads as **clinical, gentle, and specific**. The examples below are the pattern the best practices share.

| Trait | Do | Do not |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical precision | Name the condition and the treatment by their medical names, then explain once in plain words: "vasomotor symptoms (hot flashes and night sweats)". | Slang for symptoms, vivid distress, dramatizing ("hot flashes that wreck your sleep", "white knuckle through it", "floods through protection"). |
| Gentleness | Reassure by being exact: "Most treatments begin working within a few weeks." | Blunt negatives and commands: "not embarrassments", "deserve answers, not a shrug", "never a chatbot". |
| Warm formality | "You" and "we". Complete sentences. Calm verbs: evaluate, review, adjust, arrange, prescribe when appropriate. | Exclamation points, jokes, "real" or "actually" as intensifiers ("real treatments", "moisturizers that actually help"). |
| Patient-centered | Headlines state the patient's outcome or question: "Relief from hot flashes and night sweats, guided by a specialist." | Headlines about the doctor or the practice's virtues. |
| Honest | "when clinically appropriate", "many women", "results vary". A video visit is a medical evaluation, never a guaranteed prescription. | "Get your prescription today", "guaranteed relief", counts or claims that cannot be sourced. |
| Respectful | "women", "patients", "you". | "ladies", "girls", "gals", anything that comments on a woman's body or age. |

## Terminology

Say the term on the left. The right column is what it replaces.

| Say | Instead of |

| clinician, your clinician, a board certified ObGyn | our doctors, the doctor, he |
| video visit, telehealth visit | video call, chat, consult |
| care plan, written care plan | treatment you keep, game plan |
| evaluation, review | checkup, look |
| what you are experiencing, your concern, your symptoms | what feels off, what is going on with you |
| perimenopause and menopause | the change, "meno" |
| vasomotor symptoms (hot flashes and night sweats) | hot flashes that wreck your sleep |
| menopausal hormone therapy (MHT), then hormone therapy | HRT, hormones |
| genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM), vaginal dryness | down-there dryness |
| pain with intimacy; dyspareunia (pain during sex) in clinical lists | painful sex (headlines), sex problems |
| low sexual desire | low libido issues |
| heavy menstrual bleeding | flooding, heavy periods (clinical lists) |

| contraception (clinical lists); birth control (headings and search terms) | the pill (as a category) |
| polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) | PCOS alone on first mention |
| self-pay practice, transparent pricing, we do not bill insurance, a superbill is available on request | cash only, no insurance, insurance not accepted |

| emergency care, call 911 | ER, rush to the hospital |

| the women in our care, our patients | the women he treats, his patients |

Style points: "board certified" without a hyphen, "ObGyn" as written, "follow-up visit" with a hyphen when it modifies a noun, numbers under one hundred spelled out in prose, prices as figures ($249).

## Structure

- **Eyebrow**: the clinical category ("Perimenopause and menopause", "Contraception"), never a slogan.
- **Headline**: the patient's outcome or question, in a full sentence or a balanced phrase. One emphasized word at most.
- **Lede**: what the concern is, that it is treatable, what the visit involves. Three sentences.
- **Symptom lists**: "You may notice", "Reasons women schedule this visit". Each item is a clinical symptom in plain words.
- **What the visit involves**: four steps written to the patient ("Your history is reviewed before the visit", "Treatment matched to your risks and preferences").
- **Buttons**: an action the patient takes ("Schedule a video visit", "Begin your visit", "See visit pricing"). Never "Book now" alone.
- **Reviews**: "Patient reviews", "What patients say", with Google attribution. Reviews are quoted verbatim; the section heading never centers the doctor.
