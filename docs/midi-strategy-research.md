# Research: Midi Health Playbook and Signup Conversion

Compiled 2026-08-24. Applied to SheWellRX with adjustments for our cash pay, single physician model.

## Midi's positioning formula
- Emotional, dignity based headline ("THE CARE YOU DESERVE") + a verbatim patient voice subhead ("My brain won't shut up and my jeans won't zip up.")
- Blameless villain: "It's not you. It's your hormones."
- Coverage/pricing model is part of the value proposition itself. For us: "One flat fee. No insurance needed. No surprise bills." becomes the headline differentiator.

## Homepage section order (theirs, adapted for us)
1. Hero with patient voice quote and Book CTA
2. Symptom scenario cards mapped to treatments (merchandise the care plan before booking)
3. Outcome stat + trust band (for us: Google review rating for Dr. Copur instead of insurer logos)
4. Three pillars (expert led, personalized, transparent flat fee)
5. Clinician credibility wall with named credentials and human quotes
6. Stats band
7. Testimonials in symptom specific language
8. FAQ + closing CTA banner
9. Mega footer with per symptom pages (SEO)

## Funnel teardown (their registration app)
Six screens under 5 minutes: 1) visit reason (segmenting question first), 2) account (zero clinical questions), 3) contact verification, 4) payment model choice with exclusions disclosed early, 5) card on file, charged only after visit, pricing restated, 6) pick a time. ALL medical history deferred to after booking.

**Rule for us: the marketing site books, the portal intakes.** Never ask clinical questions before commitment.

## Pricing presentation
- Two column table, each row describes what the visit includes next to its price
- Plain bullets: HSA/FSA accepted, what is not included (labs, meds), no membership fee
- Pre signup cost clarity is itself a trust signal

## Conversion evidence (general research)
- Median healthcare landing page conversion ~3.8%, top performers 8 to 10%
- Trust elements adjacent to the CTA beat design polish; one test doubled form completion by moving Google review quotes next to the booking form
- 60.5% of healthcare consumers read online provider reviews (NRC Health, n=155,603); 84% check reviews before choosing a provider; 51% read six or more
- Stanford Web Credibility: show a real organization (address, real clinician photos with credentials), make claims verifiable, keep content current
- Forms: 5 or fewer fields roughly doubles conversion; multi step with 2 to 3 fields per step beats one long form
- Cost transparency answers the dominant objection (surprise bills) before it forms
