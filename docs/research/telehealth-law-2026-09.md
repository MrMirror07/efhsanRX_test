## Executive summary: the 15 things that matter most

1. **Jurisdiction is the patient's location.** NJ-licensed clinicians may treat only patients physically in NJ unless licensed where the patient is. Record the patient's originating site at every encounter (N.J.A.C. 13:35-6B.5). Dr. Copur can use the **IMLC** (NJ is a member since 2022) to add states quickly; the CNM cannot (the Nurse Licensure Compact covers RN licenses only).
2. **A questionnaire-only or phone-only first visit is not a lawful basis to prescribe in NJ.** Statute bars prescribing "based solely on the responses provided in an online static questionnaire" without an established relationship; audio-only "in isolation" is not telemedicine. Video (or audio + store-and-forward when the clinician documents that the standard of care is met) is required (N.J.S.A. 45:1-61, -62; N.J.A.C. 13:35-6B.2, -6B.5, -6B.6).
3. **Telehealth notice must be signed and dated by the patient**, and the HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices must have a written acknowledgment of receipt (N.J.A.C. 13:35-6B.9). A footer clickwrap is not enough; capture e-signatures in OptiMantra before the first visit.
4. **At the moment of booking**, NJ requires (a) notice that the visit may be with a non-physician and that the patient may request a physician, and (b) the clinician's identity, credentials, and contact information (N.J.S.A. 45:1-62). Build this into the OptiMantra booking flow and confirmation email.
5. **Controlled substances:** NJ now (since Feb 17, 2026, refined by A4852 signed July 8, 2026) requires an in-person exam before any Schedule II via telehealth, with narrow exceptions. Federally, DEA flexibilities run through **Dec 31, 2026**; a Special Registration final rule is at OMB (expected ~Nov 2026). Keep the practice non-controlled by default; if testosterone or sleep aids will be prescribed, follow Ryan Haight + DEA + NJ CDS/NJPMP rules and plan for post-2026 changes.
6. **NJ has no all-prescription e-prescribing mandate** (bills A1853/S390 never enacted). E-prescribe anyway via OptiMantra; any paper Rx must be on NJ Prescription Blanks; EPCS rules (21 CFR 1311) apply to any controlled e-Rx.
7. **HIPAA:** BAAs with every vendor that touches PHI (OptiMantra covers video, e-sign, SMS/email reminders, e-Rx, and its Zoom integration; also any migration vendor, email provider, IT/MSP, backup, shredding). Keep the marketing site PHI-free because **Cloudflare signs BAAs only for Enterprise customers**. Do not wire the interim `/book` form (it collects a health "reason for visit") to any non-BAA form processor.
8. **Tracking pixels:** After *AHA v. Becerra* (N.D. Tex. June 2024; HHS dropped its appeal), a pixel on an unauthenticated marketing page is not per se a HIPAA violation, but the rest of the OCR bulletin stands, the FTC (GoodRx, BetterHelp, NextMed) treats sharing health-inference data with ad platforms as unfair/deceptive, and the NJ Data Privacy Act treats non-PHI health data as "sensitive" (opt-in) if thresholds are met. Keep pixels off booking/portal/consent pages and never pass symptom or clinician selections to Google/Meta.
9. **Good Faith Estimate (No Surprises Act):** every uninsured/self-pay patient must get a written GFE upon scheduling (1 business day if scheduled ≥3 days out; 3 business days if ≥10 days out) or on request, with required content and disclaimers, plus a posted notice of GFE availability on the website and at scheduling (45 CFR 149.610). A pricing page is not a GFE.
10. **Medicare:** menopause patients are often 65+. To charge Medicare beneficiaries privately, Dr. Copur **and** the CNM must each file a Medicare **opt-out affidavit** and sign private contracts with each beneficiary (42 CFR 405.410-.425); otherwise refuse Medicare beneficiaries or bill Medicare. "We don't take Medicare" on the website does not achieve this.
11. **NJ advertising rules:** "board certified" claims must conspicuously name the certifying board (ABOG) and the recognizing body (ABMS); testimonials have mandated disclosures; "guarantee of results" is misconduct; keep copies of every ad for 3 years (N.J.A.C. 13:35-6.10). Calling a CNM one of "our doctors" is misleading under the same rule and FTC Section 5.
12. **Records:** keep treatment records 7 years from the last entry (N.J.A.C. 13:35-6.5); when the physical practice closes, notify patients seen in the prior 6 months, publish a newspaper notice monthly for 3 months, send the notice to the BME, and name the permanent records location. Give written termination notice to any patient whose care (obstetrics, procedures) will not continue (N.J.A.C. 13:35-6.22).
13. **Migrating 36 GB of legacy records:** BAA with the migration vendor and old EHR vendor, encryption in transit and at rest, chain-of-custody log, verification, then NIST 800-88 sanitization of source media; keep the legacy export as an immutable archive. OptiMantra offers free import (CCDA/CSV/PDF).
14. **The CNM (Nurefsan Copur):** licensed by the **Board of Medical Examiners' Midwifery Liaison Committee** (not the Board of Nursing; the consent page's complaint-routing sentence is wrong). She must practice under written **clinical guidelines with a consulting NJ physician** (N.J.A.C. 13:35-2A.6), needs BME prescriptive authorization and a formulary in those guidelines (13:35-2A.14), a written joint protocol + NJ CDS/DEA registration for any controlled substances, and her well-woman scope covers "gynecological and primary health care screening, assessment and treatment; and contraceptive services" (13:35-2A.13). The 2026 APN independence law (S2996) does **not** change the midwifery rules.
15. **Entity/telehealth-organization questions to close before launch:** SheWellRX must be a professional practice entity owned by licensees with a name that is licensee surnames or "reasonably descriptive" (N.J.A.C. 13:35-6.16); update practice location with the BME within 21 days (13:35-6.19), NJ CDS/DEA/NPPES addresses; and get written confirmation from NJ DOH that a solo, end-user telehealth practice is not a "telemedicine or telehealth organization" required to register ($1,500/yr).

## 1. State licensure and telehealth practice rules (New Jersey)

### 1.1 Which state's law applies
- **Rule:** The patient's physical location at the time of service determines which state's licensure and telehealth rules apply. New Jersey requires any provider using telemedicine with a NJ patient to "be validly licensed, certified, or registered ... to provide such services in the State of New Jersey," remain subject to NJ board regulation, comply with liability-insurance requirements, and remain subject to NJ jurisdiction. ([N.J.S.A. 45:1-62](https://codes.findlaw.com/nj/title-45-professions-and-occupations/nj-st-sect-45-1-62/); [CCHP NJ summary](https://www.cchpca.org/new-jersey/))
- **Out-of-state patients:** Treating a patient physically in NY, PA, FL, etc. requires a license in that state (each state's practice act). NJ licensure does not travel with the patient.
- **Action (REQUIRED):** Verify and record the patient's originating site before every encounter (see 1.4). Decline or reschedule any visit where the patient is outside NJ unless the treating clinician is licensed there. The site already says this; the intake must enforce it.
- **Action (BEST PRACTICE):** Add a "Where will you physically be during this visit?" field to booking and re-confirm at the start of each video visit; document it in the note.

### 1.2 Interstate Medical Licensure Compact (IMLC)
- **Rule:** NJ joined the IMLC in 2022 ([N.J.S.A. 45:9-6.2](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-45/section-45-9-6-2/); [NJ BME IMLC page](https://www.njconsumeraffairs.gov/bme/Pages/Interstate-Medical-Licensure-Compact.aspx); [IMLCC participating states](https://imlcc.com/participating-states/)). A physician whose state of principal license is NJ can obtain expedited full licenses in other member states. **Note:** New York is not an IMLC member as of the research date (VERIFY at imlcc.com before planning); Pennsylvania, Connecticut and Delaware are.
- **The CNM cannot use the IMLC.** The Nurse Licensure Compact (NJ is a member) covers RN/LPN licenses only, not APRN/CNM practice; the APRN Compact is not yet operational. She would need a separate midwifery license in each state (VERIFY per state).
- **Action (BEST PRACTICE):** If expansion is planned, apply through IMLCC for Dr. Copur; budget separate state-by-state licensure for the CNM.

### 1.3 NJ telehealth standard of care and establishing the relationship
- **Rule:** Telehealth is held to "the same standard of care or practice standards as are applicable to in-person settings." Before providing services, the licensee must determine whether telemedicine can meet the in-person standard for that encounter and, if not, advise the patient to obtain in-person care ([N.J.A.C. 13:35-6B.3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6B-3)).
- **Rule (relationship):** Before providing telemedicine, the licensee must (1) identify the patient by name, date of birth, phone number, and address (optionally SSN/insurance ID); (2) disclose the licensee's identity, license, title, specialty and board certifications; (3) review the patient's medical history and available records; (4) determine telehealth can meet the standard of care; and (5) offer the patient the opportunity to sign a consent authorizing release of records to the patient's primary care provider ([N.J.A.C. 13:35-6B.4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6B-4)). The statute adds that for initial encounters, the provider must review medical history and records "prior to initiating contact with the patient" ([N.J.S.A. 45:1-62](https://codes.findlaw.com/nj/title-45-professions-and-occupations/nj-st-sect-45-1-62/)).
- **Rule (notices at booking):** "At the time the patient requests health care services to be provided using telemedicine or telehealth, the patient shall be clearly advised that the ... encounter may be with a health care provider who is not a physician, and that the patient may specifically request that the ... encounter be scheduled with a physician." Provider identity, credentials, and contact information must be made available at scheduling or on confirmation ([N.J.S.A. 45:1-62](https://codes.findlaw.com/nj/title-45-professions-and-occupations/nj-st-sect-45-1-62/)).
- **Rule (after the visit):** Information must be entered in the medical record and, if the patient requests, forwarded to the patient's PCP or other providers ([N.J.S.A. 45:1-62](https://codes.findlaw.com/nj/title-45-professions-and-occupations/nj-st-sect-45-1-62/); [13:35-6B.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6B-5)).
- **What SheWellRX must do (REQUIRED):**
  - OptiMantra booking page and confirmation email: state that visits may be with a CNM, that the patient may request the physician, and list each clinician's name, license type/number, specialty, board certification, and practice contact info.
  - Intake (before the visit): collect full identifiers (name, DOB, phone, address) and a photo ID upload; the intake must be completed and reviewed by the clinician **before** the first visit (the current "intake after booking, clinician reads it before the visit" flow matches the statute; make it a hard gate).
  - Include a "release to my PCP" consent option in the e-signed packet.
  - Document in each initial note the determination that telehealth is appropriate; if not, refer in person and refund per the posted policy.

### 1.4 Patient location, technology, audio-only, and asynchronous visits
- **Rule (originating site):** "Prior to providing services through telemedicine or telehealth, a licensee shall determine the patient's originating site and record this information in the patient's record" ([N.J.A.C. 13:35-6B.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6B-5)).
- **Rule (technology):** Telemedicine must use "interactive, real-time, two-way communication technologies" that include a video component, **except** that the licensee may omit video when the licensee determines the standard of care can be met using "interactive, real-time, two-way audio in combination with asynchronous store-and-forward technology" ([13:35-6B.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6B-5)). The statute permits asynchronous store-and-forward only if, after reviewing the patient's records, the provider determines the in-person standard of care can be met and "informs the patient of this determination at the outset" of the encounter ([N.J.S.A. 45:1-62](https://codes.findlaw.com/nj/title-45-professions-and-occupations/nj-st-sect-45-1-62/)).
- **Rule (what is NOT telemedicine):** "the use, in isolation, of audio-only telephone conversations, electronic mail, instant messaging, phone text, or facsimile transmission" ([N.J.S.A. 45:1-61 via CCHP](https://www.cchpca.org/new-jersey/); [N.J.A.C. 13:35-6B.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6B-2)).
- **Answer to "can the first visit be audio-only or questionnaire-only?"**
  - Questionnaire-only: **No** for prescribing (see 1.5) and, practically, no for establishing the relationship (a static questionnaire is not "interactive, real-time").
  - Audio-only alone: **No** (excluded from the definition of telemedicine).
  - Audio + store-and-forward (phone call plus intake forms/photos/records) for a first visit: **Permitted only if** the clinician determines and documents that the standard of care is met without video and tells the patient at the outset. For initiating hormone therapy or contraception in a new patient, video is the defensible default.
- **What SheWellRX must do (REQUIRED):** Video for initial visits as the site already promises; a documented exception pathway (audio + store-and-forward) for technology failures; record the originating site each time.

### 1.5 Prescribing via telehealth (state layer)
- **Rule:** A licensee may prescribe via telemedicine if consistent with the in-person standard of care. "Unless the provider has established a proper provider-patient relationship with the patient, a provider shall not issue a prescription to a patient based solely on the responses provided in an online static questionnaire" ([N.J.S.A. 45:1-62](https://codes.findlaw.com/nj/title-45-professions-and-occupations/nj-st-sect-45-1-62/); [N.J.A.C. 13:35-6B.6](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6B-6)).
- **Rule (Schedule II):** Telehealth prescribing of Schedule II CDS is authorized "only after an initial in-person examination of the patient ... and a subsequent in-person visit with the patient shall be required every three months." The COVID waivers (DCA-AO-2020-15 / DCA-W-2020-14) that suspended this expired Feb 16, 2026 (EO 415 ended the state of emergency; EO 13 extended some waivers only to April 2, 2026). The Division of Consumer Affairs' modified guidance (March 2026) gave existing Schedule II telehealth patients until May 16, 2026; new patients were subject to the in-person rule from Feb 17, 2026 ([Nixon Peabody, Feb 20, 2026](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2026/02/20/new-jersey-mandates-in-person-examinations-for-schedule-ii-controlled-substances); [Nixon Peabody, Mar 9, 2026](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2026/03/09/new-jersey-issues-modified-guidance-on-prescribing-requirements); [Norris McLaughlin on EO 13](https://norrismclaughlin.com/hclb/telemedicine/new-jersey-extends-telemedicine-flexibilities-but-only-until-4-2-2026/); [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/new-jersey-telehealth-schedule-ii-prescribing-requirements/)).
- **Rule (2026 amendment):** A4852, signed July 8, 2026, keeps the in-person default but adds exceptions: adults on Schedule II **stimulants** may have the initial exam by telehealth if an in-person visit follows within 30 days, with quarterly follow-ups by telehealth and at least one in-person visit per year; patients in cancer/hospice/palliative care, long-term care residents, MAID patients, and SUD treatment patients are exempt; the minor-stimulant exception remains ([Nixon Peabody, July 16, 2026](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2026/07/16/nj-expands-telemedicine-rules-for-schedule-ii-controlled-drug-substances)). VERIFY the public-law chapter and effective date on njleg.gov.
- **Schedules III–V (testosterone, zolpidem, benzodiazepines):** No NJ in-person rule beyond the standard of care and CDS/NJPMP rules; federal Ryan Haight/DEA rules apply (Section 4).
- **NJ CDS registration and NJPMP:** Prescribing any CDS in NJ requires a NJ CDS registration (prerequisite to DEA registration) at an actual NJ location (no PO box) ([NJ Drug Control Unit FAQ](https://www.njconsumeraffairs.gov/dcu/Pages/FAQregistration.aspx)); NJPMP lookups are mandatory the first time a Schedule II or any opioid is prescribed for pain and the first time a benzodiazepine is prescribed, then quarterly while it continues ([N.J.S.A. 45:1-46.1](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-45/section-45-1-46-1/)).
- **What SheWellRX must do:**
  - REQUIRED: Never prescribe from the intake alone; every prescription follows a live visit with a documented relationship.
  - REQUIRED: No Schedule II via telehealth (none is expected in this practice).
  - REQUIRED if any CDS will be prescribed (testosterone, sleep aids): current NJ CDS + DEA registrations at a NJ address for each prescriber, NJPMP registration and lookups, EPCS with two-factor authentication.
  - BEST PRACTICE: Written prescribing policy stating the practice does not prescribe Schedule II and lists the limited III–V it will consider, with the workflow above.

### 1.7 NJ "telemedicine or telehealth organization" registration (DOH)
- **Rule:** Each "telemedicine or telehealth organization," defined as a corporation, sole proprietorship, partnership, or LLC "organized for the primary purpose of administering services in the furtherance of telemedicine or telehealth," must register annually with the NJ Department of Health ($1,500 nonrefundable fee), file an annual report (per-encounter race/ethnicity, diagnosis codes, E/M codes, payment source), notify DOH 30 days before closure, and faces $1,000/day penalties if unregistered ([N.J.S.A. 45:1-64](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-45/section-45-1-64/); [N.J.A.C. 8:53-2.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-8-53-2-1); [Riker Danzig summary](https://riker.com/blog/health-care-law/new-jerseys-doh-adopts-registration-rules-for-telehealth-and-telemedicine-organizations/); [DOH adoption notice, 53 N.J.R. 8(2)](https://nj.gov/health/legal/documents/adoption/Registration%20Standards%20for%20Telemedicine%20and%20Telehealth%20Organizations.pdf)).

SheWellRX is an end-user of OptiMantra, which points to "not required," but it has no physical location and is organized solely to deliver telehealth, which is the literal statutory definition. **VERIFY.**
- **Action (REQUIRED to resolve):** Email TTRequests@doh.nj.gov describing the practice (solo physician + CNM, NJ-licensed, OptiMantra end-user, no physical office) and request a written determination; keep it on file. If required, register before the first visit.

### 1.8 Other NJ practice-structure items triggered by going virtual
- **Practice entity and name:** Medical practices must be owned solely by licensed health professionals and organized as a solo practice, partnership, professional association/LLC, etc.; a practice name must be "composed of the actual last names of one or more of the owning licensees ... or composed of a phrase or words reasonably descriptive of the type of professional practice"; in multi-discipline practices, materials must designate each practitioner's field ([N.J.A.C. 13:35-6.16](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6-16)). **Action (REQUIRED):** Confirm SheWellRX is a professional entity (or a registered alternate name of FirstChoice ObGyn LLC) owned by Dr. Copur (a CNM co-owner is permitted as a "closely allied" profession); use "SheWellRX" together with the clinicians' names and credentials on the site and forms; VERIFY with counsel whether the trade name must be filed with the BME.
- **Practice location changes:** Report changes to the name/address of all practice locations to the BME within 21 days ([N.J.A.C. 13:35-6.19](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6-19)); failure to update the address of record is grounds for discipline. Also update the NJ Health Care Profile (physician profile) ([N.J.S.A. 45:9-22.21 et seq.](https://www.njdoctorlist.com/NJPublic/components/documents/profilestatute.pdf)), NPPES within 30 days ([45 CFR 162.410](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/section-162.410)), NJ CDS and DEA registrations (location-specific), and the malpractice carrier.
- **Malpractice insurance:** NJ physicians must maintain medical malpractice liability coverage (commonly cited as $1M per occurrence/$3M aggregate or a $500,000 letter of credit) under N.J.S.A. 45:9-19.17, and the telehealth statute expressly conditions telehealth on compliance with liability-insurance requirements. **Action (REQUIRED):** Notify the carrier of the telehealth-only model and confirm the policy covers telemedicine and the CNM. VERIFY current statutory amounts.
- **Termination/abandonment:** A licensee-patient relationship exists for any patient seen in the prior year; terminating it requires written notice and reasonable assistance finding another provider ([N.J.A.C. 13:35-6.22](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6-22)). Obstetric and procedural patients of the physical practice must be transitioned this way (Section 8).

## 2. Telehealth informed consent

- **Federal:** No federal telehealth consent statute for self-pay practices; HIPAA requires the NPP and acknowledgment (Section 3).
- **NJ rule (telehealth notice, written and signed):** Before treatment, the licensee must give the patient notice of "the risks and benefits of being treated through telemedicine or telehealth" and "how to receive follow-up care or assistance in the event of an adverse reaction to treatment or in the event of inability to communicate as a result of a technological or equipment failure," and must obtain "a signed and dated statement indicating that the patient received this notice" ([N.J.A.C. 13:35-6B.9](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6B-9)). If telemedicine cannot provide needed clinical information, the licensee must tell the patient and advise an in-person evaluation.
- **NJ rule (privacy practices):** The licensee must maintain written privacy practices consistent with 45 CFR Parts 160 and 164, provide them, and "obtain the patient's written acknowledgement of receipt of the notice" ([13:35-6B.9](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6B-9)).
- **NJ rule (release to PCP):** Offer a consent to release records to the patient's PCP ([13:35-6B.4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6B-4)).
- **NJ rule (booking notices):** Non-physician notice and clinician identity/credentials at scheduling ([N.J.S.A. 45:1-62](https://codes.findlaw.com/nj/title-45-professions-and-occupations/nj-st-sect-45-1-62/)).
- **Recommended contents of the e-signed Telehealth Consent** (the existing web page is a good base; move its substance into an OptiMantra e-sign form):

## 4. Federal prescribing rules via telehealth

### 4.1 Ryan Haight Act and DEA status
- **Rule:** The Ryan Haight Act requires at least one in-person medical evaluation before prescribing a controlled substance "by means of the Internet," unless the prescriber is engaged in "the practice of telemedicine" as defined by exceptions or by DEA special registration ([21 U.S.C. 829(e)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/829)).
- **Current flexibility:** DEA/HHS's **Fourth Temporary Extension** (published Dec 31, 2025) allows DEA-registered practitioners to prescribe Schedule II–V via audio-video telemedicine (and III–V buprenorphine via audio-only) without a prior in-person evaluation **through Dec 31, 2026** ([Federal Register 2025-24123](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/31/2025-24123/fourth-temporary-extension-of-covid-19-telemedicine-flexibilities-for-prescription-of-controlled); [DEA press release](https://www.dea.gov/press-releases/2025/12/31/dea-extends-telemedicine-flexibilities-ensure-continued-access-care); [HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/dea-telemedicine-extension-2026.html)).
- **Special Registration rule:** The Jan 17, 2025 NPRM proposed three special registrations (Telemedicine Prescribing for III–V; Advanced for II–V for specialists; Platform), each tied to a State Telemedicine Registration for every state where patients are located, plus PDMP checks, identity verification, EPCS, audio-video, data reporting, and record retention ([Federal Register NPRM](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/17/2025-01099/special-registrations-for-telemedicine-and-limited-state-telemedicine-registrations); [Bradley summary](https://www.bradley.com/insights/publications/2025/05/dea-proposed-rule-for-special-registrations-for-telemedicine-and-limited-state-registrations)). The **final rule entered OMB review on Aug 25, 2026 and is forecast for ~November 2026** ([Alliance for Connected Care tracker](https://connectwithcare.org/dea-prescribing-of-controlled-substances/)). Expect either a final rule or a fifth extension before Dec 31, 2026.
- **State law is stricter and controls:** NJ's Schedule II in-person rule applies regardless of DEA flexibilities (Section 1.5).
- **What SheWellRX must do:**
  - REQUIRED if prescribing any CDS by telehealth: DEA + NJ CDS registration for each prescriber at a NJ address; audio-video (never audio-only) for controlled prescriptions; NJPMP check per NJ law; EPCS with DEA-compliant two-factor ([21 CFR Part 1311](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-II/part-1311)); identity verification; documentation of the telemedicine exception relied on.
  - BEST PRACTICE: Decide now whether testosterone (Schedule III) for hypoactive sexual desire and hypnotics/benzodiazepines (Schedule IV) are in scope. If yes, prepare for a special-registration application in late 2026/2027 and build a referral pathway to in-person care in case flexibilities lapse.
  - BEST PRACTICE: Update the consent's controlled-substance sentence to state the actual policy (e.g., "We do not prescribe Schedule II medications by telehealth and prescribe other controlled medications only when a live video visit, identity verification, and NJ prescription-monitoring checks have been completed.").

### 4.2 Non-controlled prescribing (hormones, contraceptives, non-hormonal menopause drugs)
- **Rule:** No federal in-person requirement. Governed by NJ standard of care, the relationship/questionnaire rules (Section 1.5), FDA labeling, and the prescriber's professional judgment. Estradiol, micronized progesterone, vaginal estrogen, combined/progestin-only contraceptives, fezolinetant, paroxetine, gabapentin (not federally scheduled; not scheduled in NJ) are non-controlled.
- **Compounded bioidentical hormones:** FDA does not approve compounded products; compounding must be patient-specific under Section 503A or from a 503B outsourcing facility; NASEM (2020) found insufficient evidence for most cBHT and FDA has flagged them for potential difficult-to-compound listing ([FDA cBHT page](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/compounded-bioidentical-hormone-therapy)). BEST PRACTICE: default to FDA-approved products; if compounding is ever used, document individualized need and do not market compounded products as FDA-approved or "safer."
- **Compounded GLP-1s (if weight-loss care is ever added):** Tirzepatide left the FDA shortage list Dec 19, 2024 and semaglutide Feb 21, 2025, ending the shortage-based compounding allowance; in 2026 FDA issued dozens of warning letters to telehealth marketers and (April 30, 2026) proposed removing semaglutide/tirzepatide/liraglutide from the 503B bulks list (comments closed June 29, 2026). What remains lawful: branded products, narrow 503B supply, and 503A patient-specific compounding with documented clinical need ([FDA GLP-1 compounding page](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-clarifies-policies-compounders-national-glp-1-supply-begins-stabilize); [Medscape, 2026](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/fda-proposes-end-mass-glp-1-compounding-2026a1000e06); [Pharmacy Times](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/fda-moves-to-permanently-close-the-door-on-compounded-glp-1s)). FTC's NextMed order (Dec 2025) shows the marketing risk (undisclosed costs, fake testimonials, unsubstantiated weight-loss claims). VERIFY final FDA action before any GLP-1 program.

### 4.3 E-prescribing mandates
- **NJ:** There is **no enacted NJ mandate** that all prescriptions be electronic; bills requiring it (S390 in 2020; A1853 introduced Jan 9, 2024; A4114 in 2022) were never passed ([A1853](https://pub.njleg.gov/Bills/2024/A2000/1853_I1.HTM); [S390](https://www.njleg.state.nj.us/2020/Bills/S0500/390_I1.HTM); [A4114](https://pub.njleg.gov/bills/2022/A4500/4114_I1.HTM)). ## 5. Self-pay / no-insurance rules

### 5.1 Good Faith Estimate (No Surprises Act)
- **Rule:** Providers must give uninsured or self-pay individuals (including insured patients who choose not to submit a claim) a written GFE of expected charges upon scheduling or on request. Timing: within 1 business day if scheduled ≥3 business days ahead; within 3 business days if scheduled ≥10 business days ahead; within 3 business days of a request; not required if scheduled <3 business days ahead but must be provided if requested. Content: patient name/DOB, description of the primary item/service and date, itemized list of items/services reasonably expected (including any co-providers, currently under enforcement discretion), applicable diagnosis and service codes, expected charges, provider name/NPI/TIN and state, required disclaimers (estimate may differ; patient-provider dispute resolution if billed ≥$400 over the GFE; right to request a GFE; not a contract). Must be provided in writing (paper or electronically per patient preference), retained 6 years, and a notice of GFE availability must be prominently posted on the website, in the office, and where scheduling occurs. GFEs can cover recurring services for up to 12 months ([45 CFR 149.610](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-B/part-149/subpart-G/section-149.610); [CMS GFE FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/cciio/resources/regulations-and-guidance/downloads/guidance-good-faith-estimates-faq.pdf); [CMS sample GFE](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/nsa-sample-good-faith-estimate.pdf); [CMS rules overview](https://www.cms.gov/nosurprises/policies-and-resources/overview-of-rules-fact-sheets)). Enforcement of the co-provider/co-facility inclusion remains deferred pending rulemaking ([Koley Jessen](https://www.koleyjessen.com/insights/publications/no-surprises-act-discretionary-enforcement-of-good-faith-estimate-rules-continued)).
- **What SheWellRX must do (REQUIRED):**
  - Generate a GFE automatically in OptiMantra at booking (before or with payment): $249 initial (CPT 99204/99205-range as applicable, or the practice's flat-fee code), $149 follow-up, and state that labs/medications are billed by others and are not included (or, better, list expected lab codes/charges when known).
  - Ask each patient at scheduling whether they are uninsured or choosing self-pay, and record it.
  - Post a "Good Faith Estimate available" notice on the website (pricing page and booking page) and in the portal.
  - Keep GFEs in the chart for 6 years.
  - Add the $400 dispute-resolution disclaimer language.

### 5.2 Medicare beneficiaries (opt-out and private contracts)
- **Rule:** A physician or practitioner who wants to charge a Medicare beneficiary privately for a Medicare-covered service must **opt out** by filing an affidavit with the MAC (Novitas for NJ) within 10 days of the first private contract; the opt-out lasts 2 years and auto-renews; each beneficiary must sign a compliant private contract **before** service that states the patient accepts full responsibility, Medicare fee limits do not apply, and no claims will be submitted; emergency/urgent care is excepted. CNMs are "practitioners" eligible to opt out ([42 CFR 405.400-.455](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-405/subpart-D); [405.410](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-405/subpart-D/section-405.410); [405.415](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/405.415); [405.420](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-405/subpart-D/section-405.420)). A Medicare-enrolled physician who has not opted out cannot collect cash from a beneficiary for covered services (mandatory claim submission, limiting charge); a non-enrolled, non-opted-out physician is similarly constrained.
- **Action (REQUIRED, choose one):** (a) Both clinicians opt out and OptiMantra presents a Medicare private contract to any patient who indicates Medicare coverage (including Medicare Advantage); or (b) screen out Medicare beneficiaries at booking; or (c) enroll and bill Medicare. The website statement "does not participate with ... Medicare" is not sufficient by itself. Check current enrollment status in PECOS first; if Dr. Copur is enrolled from the physical practice, opting out also affects any hospital-based services he bills.
- **Medicaid (NJ FamilyCare):** Enrolled providers cannot bill beneficiaries beyond Medicaid payment ([N.J.A.C. 10:49-7.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-10-49-7-1)); managed-care enrollees are protected from liability ([N.J.A.C. 10:74-8.7](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-10-74-8-7)). If the practice is **not** a NJ FamilyCare provider, treating a Medicaid enrollee as private pay is generally permissible only with advance written notice that the practice does not participate and the patient's agreement to pay. VERIFY with DMAHS; simplest policy is to screen Medicaid enrollees out at booking and refer them.

### 5.3 State price transparency
- **Rule:** NJ's Out-of-Network Consumer Protection, Transparency, Cost Containment and Accountability Act (P.L. 2018, c.32; N.J.S.A. 26:2SS-1 et seq.) requires health care professionals to disclose (in writing or on the website) the health plans they participate in, tell covered persons before scheduling non-emergency services that they are out-of-network and that the billed amount is available on request, and on request disclose in writing the amount or estimated amount they will bill for the service (by CPT code) ([P.L. 2018, c.32 text](https://pub.njleg.gov/bills/2018/PL18/32_.PDF); [Bressler summary](https://www.bressler.com/publication-220)).

## 6. Online questionnaire / intake rules

- **What the intake may do (REQUIRED/permitted):** Collect identity (name, DOB, phone, address, photo ID), location at time of visit, contact preferences and SMS/email consent, chief concern, history (menstrual/menopause, obstetric, screening, medications, allergies, medical/surgical with an explicit HRT-contraindication screen, family, lifestyle), pharmacy, pregnancy status, and e-signed consents (telehealth notice, NPP acknowledgment, financial agreement/GFE, PCP release). This satisfies the statutory duty to review history and records before initial contact.
- **What it may not do:** Serve as the sole basis for a prescription or a diagnosis ("online static questionnaire" prohibition) or substitute for the live encounter needed to form the relationship ([N.J.S.A. 45:1-62](https://codes.findlaw.com/nj/title-45-professions-and-occupations/nj-st-sect-45-1-62/); [13:35-6B.6](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6B-6)).
- **Asynchronous limits:** Store-and-forward may supplement (photos, prior labs, records) and may replace video only when the clinician documents that the standard of care is met and tells the patient at the outset; audio-only alone is never "telemedicine" in NJ (Section 1.4). BEST PRACTICE: initial visits, any hormone initiation, and any controlled-substance decision are live video; refills and result reviews may be portal messages or audio + records when appropriate.
- **What can't be prescribed after questionnaire only:** Anything. In particular, controlled substances additionally need audio-video under DEA flexibilities and, for Schedule II, an in-person NJ exam.
- **Emergency screening / red-flag routing (BEST PRACTICE, standard of care):** Intake logic that stops and displays 911/ER instructions for heavy bleeding with dizziness, severe pelvic/abdominal pain, chest pain/dyspnea/leg swelling (VTE on estrogen), pregnancy with bleeding or pain, suicidal ideation; contraindication screens (estrogen: history of breast/uterine cancer, VTE/stroke/MI, migraine with aura, smoking ≥35, uncontrolled hypertension, liver disease, unexplained bleeding); and routing of postmenopausal bleeding to in-person evaluation. Messages are "not monitored 24/7" language must appear in the portal too.
- **Identity verification (REQUIRED):** Name, DOB, phone, address at intake ([13:35-6B.4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6B-4)); written fraud-and-abuse protocol covering patient authentication ([13:35-6B.8](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6B-8)). BEST PRACTICE: government ID upload plus visual match on first video visit; mandatory for any controlled prescribing.
- **Minors:** Practice policy is 18+. NJ minors may consent to certain care without parents (e.g., STI care, and family-planning services in Title X/Medicaid settings; NJ has no explicit contraception-consent statute) ([N.J.S.A. 9:17A-4](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-9/section-9-17a-4/); [NJAAP confidentiality guide](https://njaap.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/New-Jersey-AYAH-Confidentiality-Guide_Final.pdf)). ## 7. Marketing and website compliance

### 7.1 FTC health-claims rules
- **Rule:** All objective claims (efficacy, "it works," outcomes, comparisons) need competent and reliable scientific evidence before dissemination; implied claims count; disclaimers must be clear and conspicuous ([FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance); [FTC Health Claims](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/health-claims)). The FTC's 2025 NextMed action targeted a telehealth company for unsubstantiated outcome claims, fake testimonials, review distortion, and undisclosed costs ([FTC NextMed](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-approves-final-order-against-telehealth-provider-nextmed-over-charges-it-used-deceptive)).
- **Site items to fix (BEST PRACTICE/required substantiation):** keep sources for "Treatment exists, it works," pricing comparisons ("about $256"; "$250 to $350"), and "four decades" (board-certified since 1989 = 37 years; use "more than 35 years"); avoid any implied guarantee ("Treatment started the same week when appropriate" is fine because of the qualifier).

### 7.2 Testimonials and reviews
- **FTC Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 CFR Part 465, effective Oct 21, 2024):** bans fake/AI-generated reviews, buying positive or negative reviews, review suppression, misrepresenting review pages, and incentives conditioned on sentiment; applies to Google reviews ([eCFR Part 465](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-465); [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)). **FTC Endorsement Guides (2023):** testimonials must reflect typical results or disclose what is typical; material connections must be disclosed ([FTC Endorsement Guides](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/federal-register-notices/guides-concerning-use-endorsements-testimonials-advertising)).
- **NJ BME rule:** Testimonials about specific procedures require conspicuous statements that the procedure may not be suitable for every patient and that risks should be discussed; paid testimonials must be labeled "COMPENSATION HAS BEEN PROVIDED FOR THIS TESTIMONIAL"; "any guarantee of results" is misconduct; copies of every advertisement (including electronic) must be retained for 3 years ([N.J.A.C. 13:35-6.10](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6-10)).

## 8. Medical records: retention, migration, closing the physical location

### 8.1 Retention
- **Rule:** "Treatment records shall be maintained for a period of seven years from the date of the most recent entry" (all formats); computerized records must carry two patient identifiers, dated and electronically "signed" entries that cannot be altered after signing, and automatic backup ([N.J.A.C. 13:35-6.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6-5)). HIPAA requires 6-year retention of policies, BAAs, NPP versions, acknowledgments, risk analyses, and GFEs (45 CFR 164.316, 164.530(j); 45 CFR 149.610).
- **BEST PRACTICE:** Retain obstetric records longer than 7 years (birth-injury claims for the child can be filed until the child's 13th birthday under N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2(b)); many NJ counsel recommend keeping OB charts until the child is at least 13, and minors' charts until age 23 or 7 years, whichever is later. VERIFY with the malpractice carrier.

### 8.2 Migrating 36 GB of legacy records into OptiMantra (compliantly)
1. **Contract:** BAA (or confirm existing BAA covers export) with the legacy EHR vendor; BAA with any migration/conversion vendor; confirm OptiMantra's BAA covers import services (OptiMantra advertises free import from CCDA, CSV/Excel, and PDF) ([OptiMantra FAQ](https://www.optimantra.com/faq)).
2. **Inventory and scope:** List record sets (charts, images/ultrasounds, labs, billing, consents, correspondence), date ranges, formats; decide what to import as discrete data vs. PDF attachments; everything within the 7-year window (and OB records per 8.1) must remain retrievable.
3. **Encryption:** In transit via TLS/SFTP or encrypted drive with separately transmitted keys; at rest with AES-256; no unencrypted USB or consumer file-sharing (Security Rule transmission and encryption specifications, 45 CFR 164.312(a)(2)(iv), (e)(2)(ii); [HHS Security Rule](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/index.html)).
4. **Chain of custody:** Written log of who handled each copy, when, hash values (SHA-256) of export files before and after transfer, and sign-offs; workforce with access listed; minimum-necessary access.
5. **Validation:** Reconcile patient counts and sample charts (e.g., 5% random) between source and OptiMantra; document the check; keep an immutable, encrypted archive of the original export (the migration can lose formatting; the archive satisfies the "unaltered record" expectation in 13:35-6.5).
6. **Disposal of source copies:** Sanitize drives/devices per NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 1 and obtain certificates of destruction from vendors ([NIST 800-88](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/88/r1/final); [HHS disposal FAQ](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/disposal-of-protected-health-information/index.html)); require the legacy vendor to return/destroy PHI per the BAA and certify.
7. **Paper charts:** Scan under the same controls (BAA with scanning vendor), verify legibility, then shred with a certificate; keep an index.
8. **Update the risk analysis** to reflect the new system, and log the migration as a documented Security Rule activity.

### 8.3 Closing the physical practice location
- **Rule (records notice):** When a licensee ceases practice at a location or anticipates an absence over 3 months, the licensee must establish a procedure for patients to obtain copies or transfer records; publish a notice of the cessation, the retrieval procedure, and the permanent records location in a newspaper of general circulation in the practice's area at least monthly for the first 3 months; submit the notice to the BME after first publication; and make reasonable efforts to directly notify every patient treated in the preceding 6 months ([N.J.A.C. 13:35-6.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6-5)). Because Dr. Copur continues to practice (virtually), this is a location closure rather than full cessation, but the site-closure and records-location notice steps are the expected way to avoid abandonment complaints. VERIFY with the BME whether the newspaper publication is required when the physician continues in practice.
- **Rule (termination of relationships):** Written notice to patients whose care will not continue (all obstetric patients, patients needing procedures/exams/ultrasound), with reasonable assistance finding another provider; no fee for records transfer when the licensee ends the relationship ([N.J.A.C. 13:35-6.22](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6-22); [13:35-6.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-13-35-6-5)).
